01 · The brief

What the client asked for

Redesign our automotive website on Squarespace: restructure the layout, refine how it looks, make the navigation and content easier to follow, and give us an assessment of the current site with recommendations.

Paraphrased from a real Upwork brief, "Squarespace Automotive Website Redesign" (October 2026). A related auto body shop brief added privacy and terms pages, ADA accessibility and Google Ads ready compliance.

What this concept delivers

  • A restructured eight-page site with a four-link navigation
  • A filterable service menu with starting-at prices
  • A five-step booking flow with validation and a review step
  • What to expect, warranty and policy content
  • Privacy, terms and accessibility pages (sample templates)
  • This assessment and a launch checklist

02 · Site assessment

What usually holds a shop site back

Keelson is fictional, so there is no real "before" site. This assessment is a composite of the issues I see most often on independent repair shop websites, rated by how much they cost in calls and bookings.

Assessment summary: six areas, typical findings and the fix in this concept
AreaTypical findingImpactRecommendation
Navigation Nine or more top-level links, services split across several pages, and booking hidden under Contact. High Four links plus a Book button that is always visible. All services on one filterable page.
Content No prices anywhere, so every question becomes a phone call. Warranty and policies missing or buried in PDFs. High Starting-at prices on every service. Process, warranty and policies on one page, in plain English.
Mobile Text over busy photos, a phone number saved as an image, and tap targets around 30 pixels. High Solid, readable sections, a sticky header with Book, 44 pixel minimum tap targets and tap-to-call in production.
Accessibility Light grey text, unlabeled form fields, images without alt text, no visible focus and no skip link. Medium, with legal risk WCAG 2.2 AA contrast, labels and linked errors on every field, visible focus, skip link and an accessibility statement.
Conversion One long contact form with 14 fields and nothing about price or what happens next. High A five-step booking flow with a running estimate, a review step and a clear confirmation.
Compliance No privacy policy or terms, contact details hard to find and opening hours out of date. Medium Privacy, terms and accessibility pages in every footer, with the same name, address and phone everywhere.

03 · Redesign decisions

Six decisions behind this layout

Each one names the problem, the decision, and how it would be built with native Squarespace 7.1 tools.

Decision 1Navigation

Problem
Visitors arrive with one of three jobs: find a price, book a time, or check that the shop is trustworthy. A long menu slows all three.
Decision
Four top-level links and a Book button that stays in view while scrolling, on desktop and mobile. Brakes, oil and tires become filters, not pages.

In Squarespace: Site Header with the header button turned on, a fixed header on scroll, and the Announcement Bar for seasonal offers.

Decision 2Content structure

Problem
Service details were spread across pages with different layouts, and prices were missing, so visitors could not compare.
Decision
One service menu with the same card for every service: what is included, typical time and starting price. About holds the story, process, warranty and policies, each with its own anchor link.

In Squarespace: A List section or Summary block with category tags, a short Code block for the filter bar, and anchor links to #process and #warranty.

Decision 3Mobile

Problem
Most repair searches happen on a phone, often at the roadside. Small text and tiny buttons lose those visitors.
Decision
Mobile-first layouts, 17 pixel body text, 44 pixel tap targets, a compact Book button in the header and a full-screen menu that keeps focus inside and closes with Esc.

In Squarespace: Section layouts checked in the mobile editor, the mobile header button, and tap-to-call links once the real number is in place.

Decision 4Accessibility

Problem
Website accessibility complaints most often involve unlabeled forms, low contrast, missing alt text and things that only work with a mouse.
Decision
WCAG 2.2 AA as the target: every color pair checked, visible focus, a skip link, one H1 per page, labeled fields with linked errors, reduced motion support and a published statement.

In Squarespace: Site Styles color themes tuned for contrast, alt text on every image, real heading tags in text blocks and a little custom CSS for focus states.

Decision 5Conversion

Problem
Long contact forms ask for everything at once and say nothing about price or next steps, so people call instead, or leave.
Decision
A five-step booking flow: vehicle, services, date and time, contact, review. A running starting estimate, inline validation, edit links on the review step and a confirmation that says what happens next.

In Squarespace: Acuity Scheduling. Each service becomes an appointment type, vehicle questions become intake form fields, and confirmations and reminders send automatically.

Decision 6Compliance and ad readiness

Problem
Ad platforms review landing pages for a clear business identity, working navigation, honest pricing claims and a privacy policy wherever personal data is collected.
Decision
Privacy, terms and accessibility pages linked from every footer, the same name, address and phone on every page, careful "starting at" price wording and no tracking without consent.

In Squarespace: Legal pages in the footer navigation, the built-in cookie banner switched on before any analytics or ad tags, and booking completion set up as the conversion.

06 · Before launch

Launch checklist

What still needs a real business behind it before a site like this goes live.

  • Real details everywhereBusiness name, address, phone and hours identical on the site, the Google Business Profile and directories.
  • Legal reviewPrivacy policy and terms reviewed by a qualified professional for the shop's state.
  • Booking connectedAcuity appointment types, durations and buffers set per service, with intake questions for year, make, model and mileage.
  • Prices confirmedStarting-at prices checked against the shop's labor guide and parts costs, with a review date every quarter.
  • Consent before trackingCookie banner on, analytics and ad tags loaded only after consent, and booking completion set as the conversion.
  • Assistive technology passScreen reader testing with NVDA and VoiceOver, zoom to 200 percent, and a keyboard-only booking from start to finish.
  • Real photographyPhotos of the team and the bays with descriptive alt text, used where they build more trust than illustrations.
  • Search basicsUnique titles and descriptions per page, a connected domain and the sitemap submitted in Google Search Console.
